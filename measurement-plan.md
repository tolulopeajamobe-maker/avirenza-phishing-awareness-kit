# Measurement and evaluation plan

## Purpose
Evaluate whether the 30-minute phishing-awareness session improves staff knowledge, confidence and safe reporting behaviour. The pilot audience is 20 fictionalised college administrative staff in the Greenfield College training scenario.

## Measurement schedule

| When | Activity | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Before training | Short baseline knowledge check using printed or facilitated examples | Anonymous score and confidence rating |
| Immediately after | Ten-question knowledge quiz and confidence rating | Aggregate score, completion rate and change from baseline |
| 30 days after | Short follow-up quiz or tabletop scenario | Knowledge retention, confidence and correct reporting choices |
| Quarterly | Review organisation-wide reporting and process indicators | Trends, response time and recurring themes |

## Core metrics and definitions

1. **Knowledge improvement:** average post-training score minus average baseline score, reported as percentage points.
2. **Quiz completion:** number completing the quiz divided by number attending, expressed as a percentage.
3. **Correct scenario response:** percentage who correctly identify the warning signs and choose a safe next step in a facilitated exercise.
4. **Reporting rate:** number of valid suspicious-message reports received through the approved channel per 100 staff per month. Interpret alongside awareness and reporting-channel availability; an increase can reflect improved reporting, not necessarily more attacks.
5. **Time to report:** median time between a staff member noticing a suspicious message and reporting it, where timestamps are available and collection is proportionate.
6. **Verification adherence:** percentage of sampled payment or supplier-change scenarios in which staff use the approved independent verification and authorisation process.
7. **Confidence:** average self-rated confidence before and after training, using the same simple scale.

For a group of 20, use aggregate results and describe small changes cautiously. Do not publish individual scores, rankings or identifiable performance reports. Combine small subgroups where necessary to reduce re-identification risk.

## Consent-first simulation policy

**No unsolicited phishing-test emails may be sent.** A simulated phishing exercise is optional and may proceed only when every intended recipient has given prior, informed, voluntary consent and management has provided written approval.

Before any opt-in simulation:
- Explain the purpose, format, date window, data collected, who can access results, retention period and how to opt out without penalty.
- Obtain explicit consent from each recipient; exclude anyone who does not consent or withdraws consent.
- Obtain written approval from management and review the plan with IT/security and the appropriate privacy/HR lead.
- Limit the exercise to the approved audience, scope and time window.
- Use benign, controlled training pages and reserved `.invalid` example domains where possible. Do not collect real passwords, one-time codes, MFA approvals, payment details or other secrets.
- Do not trigger real payments, account lockouts, malware downloads or harmful attachments.
- Avoid highly sensitive lures involving personal crises, health, payroll emergencies or family circumstances.
- Set a stop procedure and a named contact for concerns. Stop immediately if the exercise affects non-consenting people or disrupts normal operations.
- Debrief participants promptly (ideally within 24–48 hours), explain the learning points and provide a safe way to ask questions.
- Report only aggregate outcomes to managers. Do not shame, discipline or publicly identify participants based on simulation results.

A safer default for this 20-person training is a facilitated tabletop exercise using the five fictional sample emails, printed screenshots or a group discussion. This provides practice without sending test emails.

## Privacy and governance

- Collect the minimum data needed to evaluate learning.
- Prefer anonymous baseline and post-training quizzes.
- Restrict access to raw results to the designated training/IT lead.
- Set and communicate a retention period before collecting data; securely delete identifiable raw data when no longer needed.
- Use results to improve training and reporting processes, not to rank or punish staff.
- Confirm the organisation's actual reporting route and internal policies before delivery.

## Responsibilities

- **Trainer:** deliver the session, administer anonymous checks and summarise aggregate learning outcomes.
- **IT/security coordinator:** confirm the approved reporting route, review technical content and handle incident reports.
- **Management:** approve the training and any optional simulation in writing.
- **Privacy/HR contact:** review consent wording, data minimisation, retention and participant safeguards.
